## Why Japan is a harder target than the US or Germany

Japan's web is not a smaller copy of the American web. A few things trip people up:

**The big platforms are unusually aggressive.** Yahoo! Auctions, Mercari, Rakuten and the domestic ticket and lottery sites all run bot detection tuned for their own traffic patterns. Shared datacenter ranges are burned almost instantly.

**Mobile matters more.** A large share of Japanese consumer traffic comes from NTT Docomo, KDDI (au) and SoftBank connections. If your IP is clean but obviously fixed-line hosting, some retail and app flows will look wrong to you.

**Localisation is real.** Google's Japanese results, Amazon.co.jp pricing, travel fares and store-availability pages genuinely change by prefecture. Country-level "Japan" targeting gets you in the door; city-level targeting gets you the right answer.

**Sessions are short by design.** Residential IPs belong to actual subscribers, so they drop. Any provider claiming a Japanese residential IP that stays up for a week is either selling ISP proxies or not telling you something.

## What to check before you pay for Japan IPs

Four things decide whether a Japan proxy actually works for you, and none of them is the headline price per gigabyte.

1. **Does the provider actually have Japan, and at what depth?** Country-level only, or city, ZIP and ISP filters? For most Japan use cases country-level is enough. For ad verification and local price checks, it isn't.
2. **Which billing model fits your task?** Per-IP with unlimited bandwidth, or per-GB with unlimited endpoints. These are genuinely different tools. Choosing wrong is the most common way to waste money on residential proxies.
3. **Session control.** Do you get sticky sessions with a chosen duration, plus rotation on request? A sneaker bot and a price scraper need opposite settings.
4. **How long does one IP live?** On residential networks, expect hours, not days. Auto-refresh and a replacement policy matter more than any uptime percentage on a landing page.

One more, easy to skip: what's the pool size for Japan specifically? Most providers advertise a global number — 20M, 90M, 150M — that tells you nearly nothing about how many addresses you'll actually have available in Osaka on a Tuesday afternoon.
